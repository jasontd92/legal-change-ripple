MUTUAL ARBITRATION AGREEMENT

This Mutual Arbitration Agreement ("Agreement") is entered into effective January 1, 2023, by and between Meridian Mechanical Group, Inc., a Delaware corporation, including all of its subsidiaries, affiliates, and related entities (collectively, "Company"), and the undersigned employee ("Employee").

WHEREAS, Company and Employee desire to resolve any disputes or claims that may arise out of Employee's employment relationship with Company through binding arbitration rather than litigation in court; and

WHEREAS, Company and Employee acknowledge that this Agreement is a material term and condition of employment;

NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual covenants and agreements contained herein, the parties agree as follows:

1. AGREEMENT TO ARBITRATE

Except as specifically excluded in Section 3 below, Company and Employee mutually agree that any and all claims, disputes, or controversies, whether arising in contract, tort, statute, or equity, arising out of or relating to Employee's employment with Company, Employee's compensation, the termination of employment, or any other matter connected to the employment relationship, shall be exclusively resolved by final and binding arbitration before a neutral arbitrator. This includes, but is not limited to, claims for breach of contract, wrongful termination, discrimination, harassment, retaliation, wage and hour violations, breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, and any other employment-related claims. The parties acknowledge that by entering into this Agreement, they are waiving the right to pursue such claims in court and the right to a jury trial, except as provided herein.

2. COVERED CLAIMS

This Agreement applies to and covers all claims, disputes, and controversies arising out of or relating to Employee's employment with Company or the termination thereof, including, without limitation:

a. Claims for discrimination or harassment based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity, marital status, military status, or any other characteristic protected by federal, state, or local law;

b. Claims for breach of contract, whether express or implied;

c. Claims for wrongful termination or retaliatory discharge;

d. Claims for emotional distress, defamation, or fraud;

e. Claims for violation of any federal, state, or local statute, regulation, or common law, including but not limited to claims arising under the Fair Labor Standards Act, the Family and Medical Leave Act, Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the Americans with Disabilities Act, the Age Discrimination in Employment Act, the Equal Pay Act, and similar state and local laws;

f. Claims for wage and hour violations, including claims for unpaid wages, overtime compensation, meal periods, and rest periods;

g. Claims arising under the California Labor Code and any other state employment or labor law, including claims for penalties under the Private Attorneys General Act of 2004 (PAGA);

h. Claims for negligent or wrongful acts by Company or its employees;

i. Claims for breach of confidentiality agreements or non-compete agreements;

j. Any other employment-related claim or dispute not specifically excluded in Section 3.

3. EXCLUDED CLAIMS

Notwithstanding the foregoing, this Agreement does not apply to and expressly excludes the following claims:

a. Workers' Compensation Claims. Any claim for workers' compensation benefits arising under the workers' compensation laws of any state shall not be subject to this Agreement and shall be resolved through the exclusive workers' compensation remedy provided by law.

b. Unemployment Insurance Claims. Any claim for unemployment insurance benefits shall not be subject to this Agreement and shall be resolved through the appropriate state unemployment insurance agency.

c. NLRA Claims. Any charge or claim filed with or proceeding before the National Labor Relations Board under the National Labor Relations Act shall not be subject to this Agreement. However, any monetary relief sought in connection with an NLRA proceeding shall be subject to arbitration.

d. Sexual Harassment and Sexual Assault Claims. Notwithstanding any other provision of this Agreement, Employee retains the right to bring claims for sexual harassment or sexual assault in court under the Ending Forced Arbitration of Sexual Assault and Sexual Harassment Act of 2021 (EFAA). Such claims are excluded from this arbitration requirement. All other employment-related claims, including those involving non-sexual harassment or retaliation based on protected characteristics, remain subject to this Agreement.

e. Claims Before Administrative Agencies. Employee retains the right to file administrative complaints with federal, state, or local administrative agencies, such as the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission or equivalent state agencies. However, Employee agrees that any monetary recovery sought in connection with such administrative proceedings shall be pursued through arbitration as provided herein.

4. DEFINITIONS

For purposes of this Agreement:

a. "Arbitration" means the resolution of a dispute by a single neutral arbitrator selected in accordance with the rules and procedures set forth in Section 5 below.

b. "Arbitrator" means the neutral third-party arbitrator selected in accordance with the American Arbitration Association Employment Arbitration Rules and Mediation Procedures.

c. "AAA" means the American Arbitration Association, located at 120 Broadway, Floor 21, New York, New York 10271, or any successor organization.

5. PROCEDURE

a. Selection of Arbitrator. Any arbitration shall be administered by the American Arbitration Association (AAA) under its Employment Arbitration Rules and Mediation Procedures (the "AAA Rules"), which are incorporated herein by reference. The AAA Rules are available at www.adr.org or by calling the AAA at 1-800-778-7879. If the AAA Rules conflict with any provision of this Agreement, the provisions of this Agreement shall control.

b. Demand for Arbitration. Either party desiring to initiate arbitration shall submit a written demand for arbitration to the other party via certified mail, return receipt requested, to the addresses set forth below, or as otherwise agreed by the parties. The demand for arbitration must be submitted within the applicable statute of limitations period and must include: (1) the name and address of the party seeking arbitration; (2) a clear and detailed statement of the legal and factual basis of each claim; (3) a description of the remedy sought, including any monetary damages and computation thereof; and (4) the name and contact information for the party's attorney or representative, if any.

c. Arbitrator Selection. The arbitrator shall be selected in accordance with the AAA Rules. The arbitrator must be a retired judge or an attorney with at least ten (10) years of experience in employment law. The arbitrator shall have the authority to award any remedy that would be available in court under applicable law, including damages, injunctive relief, and attorneys' fees and costs where authorized by law.

d. Venue. The arbitration shall take place in the county where Employee works or resides, or in such other location as the parties may mutually agree in writing. If the parties cannot agree on a location, the arbitrator shall determine the location in accordance with the AAA Rules.

e. Hearing and Discovery. The arbitration hearing shall be conducted in accordance with the AAA Rules. Discovery shall be permitted as needed to adequately arbitrate the dispute, subject to the discretion of the arbitrator and in accordance with the AAA Rules. The parties shall have the right to conduct depositions, obtain documents, and otherwise conduct discovery as permitted by the arbitrator.

f. Delegation. The parties delegate to the arbitrator the authority to determine whether a dispute is subject to arbitration under this Agreement, including disputes regarding the interpretation or validity of this Agreement. The arbitrator shall resolve any disputes about arbitrability or the scope of this Agreement, and the arbitrator's determination shall be final and binding. This delegation is enforceable under the Federal Arbitration Act.

g. Consolidation. Multiple claims by the same employee shall be consolidated in a single arbitration unless the arbitrator determines that consolidation is not appropriate.

h. Confidentiality. Arbitration proceedings shall be conducted in private, and the parties agree to maintain the confidentiality of the arbitration proceeding, the arbitrator's decision, and the award, except as necessary to enforce the award or as required by law.

i. Decision and Award. The arbitrator shall issue a written decision and award stating the factual and legal basis for any award. Judgment on the award may be entered in any court of competent jurisdiction. The arbitrator's decision shall be final and binding, subject only to limited judicial review as provided by the Federal Arbitration Act.

6. CLASS AND COLLECTIVE ACTION WAIVER

Class and Collective Action Waiver. Employee and the Company agree to bring any claim covered by this Agreement only in their individual capacities and not as a plaintiff or class member in any purported class, collective or representative proceeding, to the fullest extent permitted by law. This waiver does not apply to representative claims under the California Private Attorneys General Act that cannot be waived under applicable law.

7. PAGA CLAIMS

Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in this Agreement, the parties agree to the following regarding claims under the California Private Attorneys General Act of 2004 (PAGA):

a. Individual PAGA Claims. Any claim by Employee under PAGA to recover civil penalties or other individual relief on behalf of themselves must be arbitrated under this Arbitration Agreement on an individual basis only.

b. Non-Individual PAGA Claims. Employee's non-individual PAGA claims shall be stayed and Employee agrees not to pursue any non-individual PAGA claims in court until after the arbitrator makes a final determination as to Employee's status as "aggrieved" under PAGA. If the arbitrator determines that Employee was "aggrieved," Employee may then pursue non-individual PAGA claims in a civil court of competent jurisdiction. The arbitrator, and not any court, shall have sole authority to determine Employee's alleged status as an "aggrieved employee" under PAGA.

c. Arbitrator Authority. The arbitrator shall have no authority to preside over any PAGA claim by Employee on behalf of any other person or joined by or consolidated with any other person's PAGA claim. Each employee must arbitrate their individual PAGA claim separately.

d. Severability. This PAGA provision shall be severable from this Arbitration Agreement. If there is a final judicial determination that this PAGA provision is invalid, unenforceable, unconscionable, void, or voidable, the PAGA action must be litigated in a civil court of competent jurisdiction rather than in arbitration. However, to the extent any portion of this PAGA provision is enforceable, that portion shall be enforced in arbitration.

8. COST AND FEES

a. Company Pays Fees. Company shall pay all AAA administrative fees and arbitrator compensation fees that exceed the amount Employee would have paid if the claim had been pursued in court, including filing fees, hearing fees, and arbitrator compensation. Employee shall not be required to pay any costs, fees, or charges, including arbitration fees, that Employee would not have paid if the claim had been brought in court. Company shall promptly pay all fees and costs within thirty (30) days of receiving an invoice from the AAA or the arbitrator.

b. Attorneys' Fees and Costs. In any arbitration, each party shall bear its own attorneys' fees and costs, except that the arbitrator may award attorneys' fees and costs to Employee to the extent permitted by applicable law, including state or federal statute.

c. Non-Monetary Relief. If Employee requests non-monetary relief such as reinstatement, the arbitrator may grant such relief without regard to Company's ability to pay arbitration costs.

9. OPT-OUT RIGHT

a. Thirty-Day Opt-Out Period. Employee has the right to opt out of and reject this Arbitration Agreement within thirty (30) calendar days of the date Employee first receives this Agreement or signs this Agreement, whichever is earlier. To opt out, Employee must send a written notice of opt-out via certified mail, return receipt requested, to the following address:

Meridian Mechanical Group, Inc.
Legal Department
2847 Industrial Boulevard
Charlotte, North Carolina 28205

b. Contents of Opt-Out Notice. The opt-out notice must be personally signed by Employee and must include Employee's name, address, date of birth, and the date of employment. An email or facsimile shall not be sufficient.

c. Effect of Opt-Out. If Employee timely opts out of this Agreement, neither Company nor Employee shall be bound by the arbitration provisions herein, and any disputes shall be resolved in court as permitted by law. The opt-out election is irrevocable once made.

d. Continued At-Will Employment. Employee's election to opt out of this Agreement does not affect the at-will nature of Employee's employment with Company or any other term or condition of employment.

10. MUTUALITY OF OBLIGATION

This Agreement is mutual and binding on both Company and Employee. Company is equally bound by the terms of this Agreement and agrees to arbitrate any claims Employee brings against Company and any counterclaims Company may have against Employee. Both parties waive the right to pursue such claims in court or before a jury.

11. GOVERNING LAW

This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the Federal Arbitration Act (9 U.S.C. § 1 et seq.). To the extent not preempted by federal law, this Agreement shall also be governed by the substantive law of the state in which Employee works or primarily performs services for Company, without regard to conflicts of law principles. Any claim regarding the interpretation or enforceability of this Agreement shall be resolved by the arbitrator.

12. SEVERABILITY

If any provision of this Agreement is found by a court or arbitrator to be invalid, unenforceable, or void, that provision shall be severed and rendered unenforceable, but all other provisions shall remain in full force and effect. The parties agree that if any provision is found unenforceable, it shall be modified to the minimum extent necessary to make it enforceable while preserving the intent of the parties. If severance or modification is not possible, the unenforceable provision shall be struck, and the remainder of this Agreement shall continue in effect. Notwithstanding the foregoing, if the Class and Collective Action Waiver provisions are found unenforceable, the remainder of this Agreement shall remain in effect.

13. AMENDMENT AND WAIVER

No amendment or modification of this Agreement shall be valid unless made in writing and signed by both Company and Employee. The failure of either party to enforce any right or provision under this Agreement shall not constitute a waiver of such right or provision.

14. NOTICES

Any notice required under this Agreement shall be in writing and delivered by hand, sent by overnight courier service, sent by certified mail with return receipt requested, or sent by email with read receipt requested. Notices to Company shall be sent to the address listed in Section 9(a) above. Notices to Employee shall be sent to the address provided by Employee or the address on file with Company's Human Resources Department.

15. ENTIRE AGREEMENT

This Agreement constitutes the entire agreement between the parties regarding the arbitration of employment disputes and supersedes all prior understandings and agreements, whether written or oral. This Agreement does not limit Employee's right to file administrative complaints with government agencies, provided that any monetary relief shall be pursued through arbitration as set forth herein.
