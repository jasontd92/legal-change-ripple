MERIDIAN MECHANICAL OF WASHINGTON, LLC
EMPLOYEE HANDBOOK ADDENDUM
WASHINGTON STATE EMPLOYMENT LAW COMPLIANCE

Effective Date: January 1, 2025

This Addendum to the Meridian Mechanical of Washington, LLC Employee Handbook ("Company") is issued to inform employees of their rights and the Company's obligations under Washington State employment law. This Addendum supplements and, where applicable, clarifies the provisions of the main Employee Handbook. In the event of any conflict between this Addendum and the main Handbook, this Addendum shall control with respect to matters governed by Washington State law. All employees working in Washington State are covered by the provisions of this Addendum.

PAID SICK LEAVE

Washington State requires that the Company provide paid sick leave to all employees. The Company complies with the paid sick leave requirements established under RCW 49.46.200 and the Washington Department of Labor and Industries rules implementing that statute.

Accrual and Availability. Employees accrue paid sick leave at the rate of one hour per thirty hours worked, or one hour per month, whichever is more generous. Paid sick leave begins to accrue on the employee's first day of employment. Employees may begin to use accrued paid sick leave on the ninetieth calendar day following the first day of employment. The Company shall provide employees with a method for tracking accrued paid sick leave that is as protective as the method used to track other forms of paid time off. Employees will receive written notice of their paid sick leave balance at the end of each pay period.

Permitted Uses. Employees may use accrued paid sick leave for the following purposes: (1) diagnosis, care, or treatment of an existing health condition, or preventive medical care, of the employee or an employee's family member; (2) the employee's need for medical diagnosis, care, or treatment, or preventive medical care, of an employee's family member; (3) sick leave related to domestic violence, harassment, sexual assault, or stalking, including obtaining services; and (4) for an employee's own illness, including mental or behavioral health conditions. For purposes of paid sick leave, "family member" means any person related by blood, marriage, adoption, or who has a significant relationship with the employee such that they may be considered family, as well as any person for whom the employee is responsible for providing or arranging healthcare.

Payment and Non-Forfeiture. The Company shall pay employees for accrued paid sick leave upon separation of employment, including termination, resignation, retirement, or other separation. Unused paid sick leave shall not be forfeited. The Company does not require employees to work on a holiday or other day off in lieu of using paid sick leave, nor does the Company impose any penalty on employees for using paid sick leave in a lawful manner.

Minimum Standards. To the extent this policy provides for a lesser amount of paid sick leave than required by Washington State law, the minimum requirements of Washington State law shall apply. Employees may not be required to work in excess of forty hours per week, and the Company shall not penalize or threaten to penalize employees for taking paid sick leave in accordance with this policy or Washington State law.

PAID FAMILY AND MEDICAL LEAVE

Washington Paid Family and Medical Leave is administered by the Washington State Department of Labor and Industries under RCW 49.86. Effective January 1, 2025, Washington State requires that both employers and employees contribute to the paid family and medical leave insurance program.

Program Eligibility and Benefits. The Company and its employees participate in the Washington Paid Family and Medical Leave program. Employees who have worked for the Company for at least twelve months and have worked at least 1,250 hours in the past twelve months may be eligible to receive paid family and medical leave benefits. Eligible employees may use paid family and medical leave for the following purposes: (1) birth of a child, or placement of a child with the employee for adoption or foster care; (2) care of a family member with a serious health condition; (3) the employee's own serious health condition; (4) qualifying exigency leave for military family members; (5) military caregiver leave; and (6) bereavement leave for the death of a family member.

Employee and Employer Contributions. Effective January 1, 2025, employee and employer contributions to the paid family and medical leave insurance program are as follows: Employee contribution: 0.4% of gross wages, subject to the annual maximum wage base as established by the Department of Labor and Industries. Employer contribution: 0.4% of gross wages, subject to the annual maximum wage base as established by the Department of Labor and Industries. The Company withholds the employee contribution from wages in accordance with applicable law. The Company's contribution is a separate business expense.

Benefits Administration. Employees must file claims for paid family and medical leave benefits through the Washington State Department of Labor and Industries. Employees may receive partial wage replacement through the insurance program. Detailed information regarding benefit amounts, claim procedures, and eligibility requirements is available from the Department of Labor and Industries. Employees who have questions about the paid family and medical leave program should contact the Department directly or visit its website at lni.wa.gov.

Job Protection. During an approved leave of paid family and medical leave, the Company shall maintain the employee's health insurance benefits. Upon return from approved leave, the employee shall be restored to the same position or to an equivalent position with equivalent pay, benefits, and terms of employment. The Company does not require employees to use accrued paid vacation, sick leave, or other paid time off in lieu of paid family and medical leave.

MEAL AND REST BREAKS

Washington State requires that the Company provide employees with meal and rest breaks in accordance with RCW 49.12. The Company complies with these requirements as follows:

Rest Breaks. Employees working more than two hours per shift shall receive at least one ten-minute paid rest break for each four-hour work period. Rest breaks must be scheduled as close to the middle of each four-hour work period as practicable. Employees shall not be required to remain on Company premises during rest breaks, unless the nature of the work requires it. Rest breaks shall be paid time and shall be counted as hours worked.

Meal Periods. Employees working six or more consecutive hours shall receive at least one meal period of at least thirty minutes during which the employee is relieved of all duties and is not required to remain on Company premises. Meal periods shall be unpaid time. Employees working more than twelve consecutive hours may be entitled to a second meal period in accordance with applicable law. The timing of meal periods shall be mutually agreed upon between the Company and the employee, but shall be scheduled as close to the middle of the employee's shift as practicable.

Exceptions. The Company may modify meal and rest break requirements only when the nature of the work or emergency conditions make compliance impractical. Any such modifications must comply with applicable Washington State law and regulations.

PAY TRANSPARENCY

Washington State requires pay transparency in accordance with RCW 49.58. The Company complies with pay transparency requirements as follows:

Pay Range Disclosure. The Company shall disclose the pay range for any position to applicants upon request prior to a conditional job offer. The pay range represents the range of compensation the Company in good faith believes it will pay for the job opening, including base wages and any other compensation offered in connection with the position. When making a determination regarding the pay for an applicant, the Company shall notify the applicant of the pay or pay range for the position prior to making a conditional job offer.

Current Employees. Employees may request, and the Company shall provide within fourteen days, the pay range for the employee's current position. Current employees may also request information regarding the annual pay increase criteria and other forms of compensation offered to employees in the same position. The Company shall provide this information in writing.

Retaliation Prohibited. The Company does not retaliate, discipline, threaten, or otherwise discriminate against any employee for inquiring about the pay ranges for the employee's current or any other position, or for discussing pay with coworkers or others. In accordance with the National Labor Relations Act and other applicable law, nothing in this policy is intended to interfere with, restrain, or prohibit employees from discussing wages, hours, or other terms and conditions of employment with coworkers or others, or from engaging in other legally protected activity.

NONCOMPETITION COVENANTS

Noncompetition covenants and related restrictive covenants are enforceable in Washington State only to the extent permitted by RCW 49.62 and the regulations promulgated thereunder by the Washington Department of Labor and Industries.

Earnings Threshold. Any noncompetition covenant entered into between the Company and an employee is enforceable only if the employee's annual compensation exceeds the earnings threshold established by the Washington Department of Labor and Industries. Effective as of January 1, 2025, the earnings threshold is $116,400 per year, adjusted annually on January 1st in accordance with adjustments to the cost of living. The Department of Labor and Industries publishes the current earnings threshold on its website.

Definition and Scope. For purposes of this policy, a "noncompetition covenant" means an agreement in which an employee covenants not to engage in a lawful profession, trade, or business that competes with the business of the Company. Any such covenant applies only to the geographic area in which the Company engages in business at the time of termination of employment and may not extend for more than six months following termination, unless the employee was terminated due to the employee's receipt of trade secrets or confidential information as defined by RCW 49.62.

Exceptions and Limitations. Notwithstanding the above, a noncompetition covenant may be enforced if: (1) the employee was terminated by the Company for any reason; (2) the employee receives at least ninety percent of the benefit of the bargain as compensation for the noncompetition covenant; and (3) the covenant meets all requirements of RCW 49.62.

Notice and Acknowledgment. The Company provides this notice regarding the enforceability of noncompetition covenants. Employees who have entered into a noncompetition covenant with the Company should contact Human Resources with any questions regarding the applicability and enforceability of that covenant, including whether their current compensation exceeds the Department of Labor and Industries earnings threshold.

Severability. If any portion of a noncompetition covenant is found to be unenforceable under RCW 49.62, the Company shall modify the covenant to the minimum extent necessary to make it enforceable under Washington law. Nothing in this policy shall be construed as creating or modifying any noncompetition covenant between the Company and an employee. All noncompetition covenants are contained in separate written agreements.
