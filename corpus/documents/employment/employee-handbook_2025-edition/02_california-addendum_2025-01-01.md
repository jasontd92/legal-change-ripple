CALIFORNIA ADDENDUM TO THE 2025 EMPLOYEE HANDBOOK

MERIDIAN MECHANICAL GROUP, INC.

Effective Date: January 1, 2025

This California Addendum to the 2025 Employee Handbook (the "Addendum") is issued by Meridian Mechanical Group, Inc., a California corporation with its principal place of business located at 2847 Industrial Boulevard, Suite 140, Sacramento, California 95838 (the "Company"). This Addendum applies to all employees who work in California and supplements the Company's general 2025 Employee Handbook. In the event of any conflict between the provisions of this Addendum and the general handbook, the provisions of this Addendum shall control with respect to employees working in California. This Addendum is effective as of January 1, 2025.

SECTION 1: MEAL AND REST PERIODS

The Company recognizes and complies with California's meal and rest period requirements as set forth in the California Labor Code and applicable Industrial Welfare Commission Orders.

1.1 Meal Periods. The Company shall provide all non-exempt employees with one paid meal period of not less than thirty (30) minutes during each work shift of more than five (5) hours. For work shifts of more than ten (10) hours, the Company shall provide a second meal period of not less than thirty (30) minutes. For work shifts of more than fourteen (14) hours, the Company shall provide a third meal period of not less than thirty (30) minutes. These meal periods shall be provided at times that do not coincide with the beginning or end of the work shift, and insofar as practicable, approximately in the middle of the work period. Employees must be fully relieved of all duty and free to leave the work premises during meal periods. The Company shall not require employees to work during meal periods.

1.2 Waiver of Meal Periods. An employee may agree to forgo a meal period only if the work shift does not exceed six (6) hours and the employee and the Company mutually agree in writing to the waiver prior to the work shift. Any such written agreement must be presented to the employee in clear and unambiguous language. Employees may revoke this agreement at any time by providing written notice to the Human Resources Department.

1.3 Rest Periods. The Company shall provide all non-exempt employees with paid rest periods during each work shift. Rest periods shall be provided at the rate of ten (10) minutes for each four (4) hours worked, or major fraction thereof. Rest periods shall be provided as close as practicable to the middle of each four-hour work period. Employees must be relieved of all work duties during rest periods and must not be required to remain at the work premises during rest breaks. The Company shall make reasonable efforts to enable employees to take rest periods, but the failure of an employee to take a rest period shall not result in additional compensation unless otherwise required by law.

1.4 Compensation for Missed Meal and Rest Periods. If the Company fails to provide a compliant meal or rest period, the employee shall be paid one (1) hour of pay at the employee's regular rate of pay for each work shift during which a meal or rest period was not provided. If multiple meal or rest periods are missed during the same work shift, the Company shall pay one (1) hour of pay for each missed meal or rest period.

SECTION 2: PAID SICK LEAVE

The Company provides paid sick leave to all non-exempt and exempt California employees in accordance with California Labor Code Section 246 and San Francisco Administrative Code Section 4.9, as applicable.

2.1 Accrual and Availability. Effective January 1, 2025, the Company shall provide all California employees with a minimum of forty (40) hours of paid sick leave per calendar year, which is equivalent to five (5) business days. This sick leave shall be accrued at a rate of not less than one (1) hour per thirty (30) hours worked. Employees shall be permitted to use accrued paid sick leave beginning on the ninety-first (91st) calendar day of employment.

2.2 Use of Paid Sick Leave. Employees may use paid sick leave for any of the following purposes: (a) diagnosis, care, or treatment of an existing health condition or preventive care for the employee or the employee's family member; (b) leave necessitated by domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking; or (c) childcare and school activities relating to the child's health, safety or education, or to enroll in or attend childcare or school. Employees shall provide reasonable notice of the need to use paid sick leave when the need is foreseeable. For unforeseeable absences, employees shall notify their immediate supervisor as soon as practicable.

2.3 Carry Over and Payment Upon Termination. Paid sick leave not used during the calendar year may be carried forward to the following calendar year up to a maximum accrual of forty-eight (48) hours. Upon termination of employment, the Company shall pay all accrued and unused paid sick leave at the employee's regular rate of pay, in compliance with California Labor Code Section 227.

2.4 Relationship to Other Leave. Paid sick leave shall be in addition to any other paid leave provided by the Company, including vacation, holidays, and other leaves of absence. Use of paid sick leave shall not result in forfeiture of any other benefit, and shall not be considered time worked for purposes of calculating overtime.

SECTION 3: LEAVES OF ABSENCE

The Company recognizes and complies with California's various leave of absence laws, including the California Family Rights Act and the Pregnancy Disability Leave Law.

3.1 California Family Rights Act Leave. Eligible employees are entitled to leave under the California Family Rights Act (CFRA), which is codified in California Government Code Sections 12945.1 through 12945.2. An employee who has been employed by the Company for at least twelve (12) months and has worked at least 1,250 hours in the twelve (12) months preceding the request for leave is entitled to up to twelve (12) weeks of unpaid leave during any twelve (12)-month period for the following purposes: (a) the birth of the employee's child or placement of a child with the employee for adoption or foster care; (b) to bond with a child; (c) to care for the employee's spouse, domestic partner, parent, child, sibling, grandparent, or grandchild with a serious health condition; (d) due to a serious health condition of the employee; (e) due to any qualifying exigency arising from the employee's spouse's, domestic partner's, or parent's military service or call to active duty status; or (f) for military caregiver leave.

3.2 Eligibility and Notice Requirements. To be eligible for CFRA leave, an employee must: (a) have been employed with the Company for at least twelve (12) consecutive months; (b) have worked at least 1,250 hours during the twelve (12) months preceding the request for leave; and (c) work at a location where the Company employs at least fifty (50) employees within seventy-five (75) miles. Employees must provide at least thirty (30) days' advance written notice of foreseeable leave. For unforeseeable leave, the employee shall notify the Human Resources Department as soon as practicable.

3.3 Pregnancy Disability Leave. Eligible female employees are entitled to leave under the Pregnancy Disability Leave Law, California Government Code Section 12945. An employee who is disabled on account of pregnancy, childbirth, or related medical conditions is entitled to up to four (4) months of unpaid leave during a twelve (12)-month period. Pregnancy disability leave is separate from and in addition to CFRA leave. The Company shall maintain health insurance benefits during pregnancy disability leave under the same terms as if the employee were actively working.

3.4 Benefits During Leave. During all protected leave periods, the Company shall continue to provide health insurance benefits and shall maintain the employee's benefits accrual, including paid time off. Upon return from protected leave, the employee shall be restored to the same position or an equivalent position with equivalent pay, benefits, and terms of employment.

3.5 Medical Certification. The Company may request medical certification to support a request for CFRA leave or pregnancy disability leave. The employee shall provide such certification within fifteen (15) days of the Company's request, or the leave request may be delayed pending receipt of the certification. All medical information shall be maintained in confidentiality in accordance with the Americans with Disabilities Act and California law.

SECTION 4: NON-COMPETITION COVENANTS

The Company does not require California employees to agree to non-competition covenants, and the Company acknowledges that any such covenant entered into with a California employee is void and unenforceable.

4.1 Void Non-Competition Agreements. Pursuant to California Business and Professions Code Sections 16600 and 16600.5, no employee shall be required to enter into an agreement that restricts the employee's right to practice any lawful profession, trade, or business. The Company does not enforce any non-competition covenant against California employees. Any non-competition agreement or clause that may exist in any employment agreement, offer letter, or other document is unenforceable against California employees and shall have no legal effect.

4.2 Choice of Law. This provision regarding non-competition covenants is governed by California law, regardless of the law selected in any underlying agreement. Employees are not subject to non-competition restrictions during employment or after termination, separation, or departure from the Company.

4.3 Acknowledgment. All California employees acknowledge their understanding that they are not bound by any non-competition covenant, and that any such restriction is void under California law. This acknowledgment shall not waive any employee's rights under California law.

SECTION 5: REIMBURSEMENT OF BUSINESS EXPENSES

The Company shall reimburse all necessary and ordinary business expenses incurred by California employees in the performance of their duties.

5.1 Eligible Expenses. Business expenses subject to reimbursement include, but are not limited to: (a) mileage and vehicle expenses for work-related travel at the current Internal Revenue Service standard mileage rate or actual vehicle expenses, whichever is greater; (b) meals consumed during work-related travel or business meetings; (c) lodging and transportation for business travel; (d) tools, equipment, and materials used in the performance of work duties; (e) continuing education and training directly related to job duties; (f) licenses, permits, and certifications required to perform work; (g) protective equipment and safety gear not provided by the Company; and (h) cellular phone or communication device expenses when required for work duties.

5.2 Submission of Reimbursement Requests. Employees shall submit all reimbursement requests to the Human Resources Department within thirty (30) days of incurring the expense, along with original receipts or itemized documentation. All requests shall identify the business purpose of the expense and the date incurred. The Company shall process reimbursements within thirty (30) days of receipt of the request and required documentation.

5.3 Expense Reporting System. Employees shall use the Company's designated expense reporting system to submit reimbursement requests. Information regarding access to the system and proper submission procedures shall be provided by the Human Resources Department. Employees may also submit reimbursement requests on the approved expense form, available from the Human Resources Department.

5.4 Deductions from Pay. In accordance with California Labor Code Section 200, the Company shall not deduct business expenses from employee wages unless the expense was incurred as a result of the employee's willful misconduct or gross negligence. All ordinary and necessary business expenses shall be fully reimbursed separate from and in addition to the employee's regular compensation.

SECTION 6: WORKPLACE VIOLENCE PREVENTION

The Company has adopted and implemented a Workplace Violence Prevention Plan in accordance with Assembly Bill 1889 (SB 553), California Code of Regulations Title 8 Section 3203.2, and other applicable California law.

6.1 Workplace Violence Prevention Policy. The Company is committed to preventing workplace violence and to providing all employees with a safe and secure work environment. Any act or threat of violence, harassment, intimidation, or other conduct that interferes with an employee's work performance or creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment is strictly prohibited. This policy applies to all employees, contractors, vendors, customers, and visitors at Company facilities and work sites.

6.2 Prohibited Conduct. Prohibited conduct includes, but is not limited to: (a) any threat or act of physical violence; (b) possession of weapons on Company property or at work sites; (c) verbal or written threats or statements expressing intent to cause bodily harm; (d) repeated harassing communication; (e) intimidating or threatening gestures; (f) any behavior that places another person in reasonable apprehension of imminent harm; and (g) stalking or surveillance of employees.

6.3 Reporting Procedures. Employees who experience, witness, or become aware of workplace violence or threats of workplace violence shall immediately report the incident to their immediate supervisor, the Human Resources Department, or the Workplace Safety Coordinator, whose office is located at 2847 Industrial Boulevard, Suite 140, Sacramento, California 95838. Employees may report incidents verbally or in writing. All reports shall be taken seriously and shall be investigated promptly and confidentially.

6.4 No Retaliation. The Company strictly prohibits retaliation against any employee who reports workplace violence in good faith, assists in the investigation of a workplace violence incident, or refuses to engage in prohibited conduct. Any employee who believes they have experienced retaliation shall immediately notify the Human Resources Department.

6.5 Investigation and Response. Upon receipt of a report of workplace violence or threats thereof, the Company shall conduct a prompt and thorough investigation. If the allegation is substantiated, the Company shall take appropriate corrective action, up to and including termination of employment or cessation of business relationships with contractors or vendors. The Company shall document all investigations and corrective actions taken. Employees involved in the incident shall be notified of the outcome of the investigation to the extent permitted by law.

6.6 Workplace Violence Prevention Training. The Company shall provide workplace violence prevention training to all employees as required by California law. Training shall cover the Company's workplace violence prevention policy, procedures for reporting incidents, and employee rights and responsibilities. New employees shall receive training within thirty (30) days of hire. All employees shall receive refresher training at least annually.

6.7 Threat Assessment. The Company maintains a process for identifying and assessing workplace violence threats and hazards. This assessment includes reviewing employees' work histories and backgrounds as permitted by law, evaluating the nature and extent of contact with potentially violent individuals, and assessing the adequacy of current safeguards and security measures.

SECTION 7: TRAINING REPAYMENT AGREEMENTS

Any training repayment obligation shall be documented in a written agreement signed by both the employee and an authorized representative of the Company.
