1. AT-WILL EMPLOYMENT

Meridian is an at-will employer. Employment with the Company is on an at-will basis, meaning that either you or the Company may terminate the employment relationship at any time, for any reason or no reason, with or without cause, and with or without notice, except where such notice is required by applicable federal, state, or local law. The at-will employment relationship applies to all employees, regardless of position, tenure, or performance.

At-will employment means that:

a) The Company may terminate your employment at any time without cause or notice;
b) You may resign from employment at any time for any reason;
c) Nothing in this handbook, no management representation, and no prior practice constitutes a contract of employment for any specific term;
d) The Company may change your position, responsibilities, compensation, benefits, work schedule, work location, and other terms and conditions of employment at any time without notice or cause;
e) No officer or employee of the Company, other than the Chief Executive Officer in a written agreement signed by the CEO and the employee, has the authority to create a contract of employment for any specific term or alter the at-will nature of employment.

Nothing in this handbook is intended to modify the at-will nature of employment, except to the extent expressly required by applicable state or federal law. All employment-related promises, representations, or statements made to you prior to, during, or after the hiring process are subject to this at-will employment policy unless they are contained in a written agreement signed by the CEO.

3. ANTI-HARASSMENT AND NON-DISCRIMINATION POLICY

Meridian is committed to providing a workplace free from harassment, discrimination, and retaliation. The Company has zero tolerance for any conduct that violates this policy.

Prohibited Conduct. The Company strictly prohibits harassment or discrimination based on race, color, national origin, sex (including pregnancy, childbirth, gender identity, sexual orientation, and sex-stereotyping), age, disability, religion, political affiliation, military status, covered veteran status, genetic information, or any other characteristic protected by law. Harassment is defined as unwelcome conduct, communication, jokes, slurs, images, advances, requests, or physical contact based on a protected characteristic that is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter the terms or conditions of employment or create an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment.

Sexual Harassment. Sexual harassment is a form of discrimination that is strictly prohibited. Sexual harassment includes, but is not limited to:

a) Unwelcome sexual comments, jokes, propositions, or advances;
b) Unwelcome physical contact of a sexual nature, including touching, hugging, or other physical conduct;
c) Displaying or discussing sexually explicit materials, images, or conversations;
d) Quid pro quo harassment, including making employment benefits, advancement, or continued employment conditional upon submission to sexual conduct;
e) Creating a hostile work environment through persistent unwelcome sexual conduct;
f) Electronic harassment, including unwelcome sexual emails, text messages, or social media messages.

Reporting Procedures. Any employee who experiences or witnesses harassment, discrimination, or retaliation is required to report it immediately. Employees may report concerns in the following ways:

a) Directly to their immediate supervisor;
b) To any member of management;
c) To the Human Resources Department;
d) By written complaint to: Meridian Mechanical Group, Inc., Human Resources Department, 4701 196th Street SW, Suite 150, Lynnwood, Washington 98036;
e) By email to: hr@meridianmechanical.com;
f) By telephone to: (425) 555-0142 ext. 105 (HR Department).

Investigation and Resolution. Upon receipt of a complaint, the Company will:

a) Conduct a prompt, impartial, and thorough investigation;
b) Maintain confidentiality to the extent possible while conducting the investigation;
c) Interview all relevant parties and witnesses;
d) Document all findings;
e) Take appropriate corrective action if harassment or discrimination is substantiated;
f) Prevent further harassment and retaliation;
g) Take disciplinary action up to and including immediate termination if violations are confirmed.

The Company will conclude investigations as promptly as possible, typically within 15 business days of receiving a complaint.

Retaliation Prohibited. The Company strictly prohibits retaliation against any employee for reporting harassment, discrimination, or participating in an investigation or complaint process. Retaliation includes adverse employment actions, negative comments about the complainant or witness, exclusion from meetings or information, or any other conduct intended to discourage the reporting of harassment or discrimination. Any employee who engages in retaliation will be subject to immediate disciplinary action up to and including termination.

External Reporting Rights. Employees also have the right to file complaints with government agencies, including:

a) The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) at www.eeoc.gov or (800) 669-4000;
b) The U.S. Department of Labor at www.dol.gov;
c) State labor agencies and fair employment practices commissions;
d) State attorneys general offices.

5. HOURS AND OVERTIME

Classification. The Company employs both exempt and non-exempt employees. Non-exempt employees are entitled to overtime compensation as required by federal and state law. Exempt employees are excluded from overtime requirements and are paid on a salary basis. Your employment classification is provided in your offer of employment or employment agreement.

Workweek. For purposes of calculating overtime, the Company's workweek runs from Monday through Sunday. Standard full-time employment is 40 hours per week. Overtime compensation is required for non-exempt employees who work more than 40 hours in a workweek, or who work more hours than required by applicable state law, whichever is more protective of the employee.

Field Technicians and Service Personnel. Field technicians are required to record all hours worked using the Company's mobile time tracking application or in-vehicle computer system. Time tracking must include service calls, travel time, training, meetings, meal breaks, and any other time for which you are performing work.

Service Call Scheduling. The Company attempts to schedule work efficiently to avoid overtime. However, emergency service calls, customer requests, weather delays, and other operational factors may require technicians to work overtime. Technicians are expected to work reasonable overtime when requested by supervisors.

On-Call Rotations and Emergency Service. Certain technicians participate in on-call rotation schedules to respond to emergency service calls. Technicians designated as on-call are required to:

a) Maintain availability to respond to emergency calls;
b) Carry communication devices (phone or radio);
c) Respond to calls within the timeframe specified (typically within 30 minutes);
d) Be in a condition to safely operate vehicles and equipment;
e) Not consume alcohol or use substances that would impair their ability to respond.

Compensation for On-Call Time. Compensation for on-call duty, including whether time spent on-call but not called to work is compensable, is determined in accordance with applicable state law and is specified in your employment agreement or offer letter. Generally, time actually worked in response to a call-out during on-call periods is compensated at the applicable overtime rate.

Premium Pay. In some instances, the Company may offer premium pay rates for work performed at certain times, in certain locations, or under certain conditions. Premium pay is determined by supervisory and management decision and does not constitute overtime unless the total hours worked exceed 40 in the workweek.

Overtime Compensation. Non-exempt employees who work overtime are compensated at one and one-half times their regular hourly rate for all hours worked in excess of 40 hours per workweek. Some states require daily overtime as well; employees in those states are compensated in accordance with applicable state law. Overtime compensation is calculated on the regular straight-time hourly rate and does not include bonuses, commissions, or other forms of compensation.

Compensable Time. All time that you are required to work or for which you are performing Company business is compensable time. This includes:

a) All service calls and customer visits;
b) Travel time to and from job sites;
c) Training and instruction;
d) Safety meetings and mandatory meetings;
e) Time waiting for customers to provide access;
f) Administrative duties and paperwork;
g) Equipment maintenance and vehicle inspections.

Non-Compensable Time. Break periods and meal periods that are truly uninterrupted and during which the employee is completely relieved from duty are not compensable time, provided such breaks are permitted by law.

Breaks and Meal Periods. The Company complies with all applicable federal, state, and local laws regarding breaks and meal periods. Non-exempt employees are entitled to breaks and meal periods as required by law. Specific break schedules depend on state law and are communicated by supervisors.

Accurate Timekeeping. All employees are required to record time worked accurately using the designated time tracking system. Failure to record time properly, falsifying time records, or allowing another employee to record your time is strictly prohibited and may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination. Time records must be submitted and approved by supervisors on a timely basis.

6. PAY PERIODS AND PAYROLL

Pay Schedule. Meridian pays employees on a bi-weekly schedule. Payday is every other Friday. The pay period runs from Monday through Sunday. Employees receive detailed pay stubs showing gross pay, withholdings, deductions, and net pay.

Automatic Deposit. All employees participate in automatic direct deposit of paychecks to the bank account of their choice. Employees are responsible for providing accurate banking information and must notify the Human Resources Department of any changes. Direct deposit payroll is processed on Thursdays and typically appears in employee bank accounts by Friday morning, depending on individual bank processing times.

Wage Deductions. The Company complies with all applicable federal, state, and local wage and hour laws. Deductions from paychecks are limited to those mandated by law or specifically authorized in writing by the employee. Authorized deductions include:

a) Federal income tax withholding;
b) State and local income tax withholding;
c) Social Security taxes;
d) Medicare taxes;
e) Court-ordered garnishments and levies;
f) Health insurance premiums;
g) Retirement plan contributions;
h) Other deductions authorized in writing.

The Company does not deduct from wages for uniforms, tools, equipment, damage to Company property, or cash register shortages unless specifically authorized by applicable law. Any deduction not permitted by law will be reviewed and corrected immediately.

Minimum Wage Compliance. The Company pays employees no less than the applicable federal, state, or local minimum wage, whichever is greater. Some positions may include incentive compensation, bonuses, or commissions in addition to the base rate.

Pay Stub Accuracy. Employees should review pay stubs carefully for accuracy. Any discrepancies should be reported to the Human Resources Department immediately. The Company will investigate and correct any errors within one pay period.

Separation and Final Paycheck. Upon separation of employment, the Company will issue a final paycheck within the timeframe required by law. The final paycheck will include payment for all earned but unpaid wages and, where required by law, accrued but unused paid time off.

7. PAID TIME OFF

Eligibility. Paid time off (PTO) is provided to eligible full-time employees. Part-time employees may be eligible for PTO on a pro-rata basis. PTO eligibility begins on the employee's hire date. Temporary, seasonal, and contract employees are not eligible for paid time off.

Accrual. Full-time employees accrue paid time off at the rate of 20 days per calendar year (160 hours). This PTO includes vacation, sick leave, personal days, and other absences. Employees may use accrued PTO flexibly subject to operational needs and supervisory approval.

Usage and Requests. Employees may use accrued PTO for any reason, including vacation, illness, personal appointments, family matters, or other purposes. Requests for PTO should be submitted to the employee's supervisor as far in advance as possible, preferably at least two weeks for vacation. Supervisors will attempt to accommodate PTO requests but may deny or reschedule requests based on operational needs, workload, or staffing requirements.

Limitations. The Company reserves the right to:

a) Limit the amount of PTO used in any pay period;
b) Deny requests for PTO based on operational needs;
c) Require that vacation time be scheduled at times determined by the Company;
d) Require employees to use PTO rather than remain unpaid during operational slowdowns.

Carry-Over Policy. PTO does not roll over from one calendar year to the next. Any unused PTO balance remaining on December 31st of each year is forfeited and not paid out. However, employees in states where "use it or lose it" policies are prohibited by law (such as California) will be governed by applicable state law. Meridian Mechanical operates under the applicable state-specific rules as outlined in the state addenda in Section 17.

Payout Upon Separation. Upon separation of employment, employees will be paid for accrued and unused PTO in accordance with applicable state law. Employees in states requiring payout of unused vacation will receive such payment with their final paycheck. Employees in states that do not require payout of unused PTO will forfeit all unused PTO upon separation.

Vehicle Assignment. Meridian provides vehicles to certain field technicians, service managers, and other personnel for business purposes. Company vehicles are the property of Meridian. Vehicles are assigned to individual employees for their exclusive use during work hours unless otherwise authorized.

Vehicle Use and Operation. Company vehicles may be used only for authorized business purposes, including service calls, customer visits, travel to job sites, equipment delivery, and other Company-related activities. Unauthorized personal use is prohibited. Employees are required to:

a) Operate vehicles safely and in accordance with all traffic laws;
b) Maintain a valid driver's license at all times;
c) Comply with all vehicle safety laws and regulations;
d) Refrain from using cellular phones while driving (unless hands-free is available);
e) Not operate vehicles while impaired by alcohol, drugs, or medications;
f) Ensure that passengers are appropriately restrained.

Vehicle Maintenance and Care. Employees are responsible for maintaining assigned vehicles in clean and safe operating condition. Routine maintenance responsibilities include:

a) Checking and maintaining proper fluid levels (oil, coolant, brake fluid, windshield washer);
b) Inspecting tire pressure and condition;
c) Ensuring that lights and wipers function properly;
d) Maintaining a clean interior and exterior;
e) Promptly reporting any maintenance needs or mechanical issues.

Failure to properly maintain a vehicle may result in disciplinary action.

GPS Telematics and Monitoring. All Company vehicles are equipped with GPS telematics systems for tracking, safety, efficiency, and loss prevention purposes. Telematics systems track vehicle location, speed, fuel consumption, engine performance, diagnostics, and driving behavior in real-time. Employees should be aware that their location, routes, and driving patterns are monitored by the Company during work hours.

Use of Telematics Data. The Company uses telematics data to:

a) Optimize routing and dispatching of service technicians;
b) Improve customer service response times and reliability;
c) Enhance driver and public safety;
d) Monitor fuel consumption and maintenance expenses;
e) Recover stolen vehicles;
f) Prevent unauthorized use of Company vehicles;
g) Investigate accidents and safety incidents;
h) Provide feedback to drivers regarding driving performance;
i) Ensure compliance with safety policies.

Driving Behavior and Safety. Telematics systems monitor driving behavior including speeding, rapid acceleration, hard braking, and excessive idling. The Company establishes safety parameters and expectations for all drivers. Unsafe driving behaviors may be addressed through:

a) Driver coaching and retraining;
b) Written warnings;
c) Suspension of driving privileges;
d) Disciplinary action up to and including termination.

Excessive speeding, reckless driving, or repeated safety violations may result in immediate suspension of driving privileges and termination of employment.

Accidents and Damage. Any accident, damage, or incident involving a Company vehicle must be reported to your supervisor immediately, regardless of the apparent severity. Failure to report an accident is grounds for discipline up to and including termination. Drivers involved in accidents are required to:

a) Ensure the safety of all persons involved;
b) Call 911 if anyone is injured;
c) Contact police to file an accident report;
d) Remain at the scene until police arrive (if required by law);
e) Collect information from the other driver(s), including name, phone number, driver's license number, insurance information, and vehicle information;
f) Take photos of vehicle damage and accident scene;
g) Obtain names and contact information of witnesses;
h) Notify the Company immediately.

The Company will investigate all accidents. Accidents caused by driver negligence, reckless operation, or violation of Company safety policies may result in the employee being held financially responsible for repair costs in accordance with state law. The Company maintains insurance coverage for accidents; however, the employee's insurance and legal responsibility will be determined based on the investigation findings.

11. SAFETY AND WORKPLACE HEALTH

Commitment to Safety. Meridian is deeply committed to the health and safety of all employees. The Company complies with all applicable Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) regulations, state workplace safety laws, and industry standards. All employees are required to participate in the Company's safety program and maintain a safe, healthy work environment.

Safety Responsibilities. All employees are responsible for:

a) Following all safety rules, policies, and procedures;
b) Using required personal protective equipment (PPE) correctly;
c) Immediately reporting safety hazards, violations, or near-misses;
d) Suggesting safety improvements;
e) Participating in safety training;
f) Not engaging in horseplay or unsafe conduct;
g) Maintaining a safe work environment.

Safety Equipment and Personal Protective Equipment. The Company provides and maintains all required safety equipment and personal protective equipment, including hard hats, safety glasses, gloves, respiratory protection, harnesses, and other equipment appropriate to the work performed. Employees are required to wear and use all prescribed PPE correctly and must not remove or bypass safety equipment.

Failure to use required safety equipment may result in disciplinary action up to and including immediate termination.

Confined Space Work. Employees working in confined spaces, including attics, crawl spaces, basements, mechanical rooms, and other restricted or enclosed areas, must comply with strict safety protocols. Confined space work may only be performed by employees who have completed the Company's confined space entry and safety training program.

Confined space hazards include oxygen deficiency, toxic gases, fire/explosion hazards, engulfment hazards, and other dangers. Employees working in confined spaces must:

a) Wear appropriate respiratory protection and ventilation equipment;
b) Use harnesses and safety lines where required;
c) Verify atmospheric conditions before entry;
d) Have rescue equipment and trained personnel standing by;
e) Follow all Company confined space procedures;
f) Never work alone in confined spaces.

Violations of confined space procedures are grounds for immediate disciplinary action.

Heat Illness Prevention. Employees working outdoors or in hot environments face the risk of heat-related illness, including heat exhaustion, heat cramps, and potentially fatal heat stroke. The Company implements the following heat illness prevention measures:

a) Providing adequate drinking water and encouraging frequent hydration;
b) Providing shaded rest areas or air-conditioned vehicles for breaks;
c) Allowing regular rest breaks in cool environments;
d) Allowing longer and more frequent breaks during extreme heat;
e) Monitoring employees for signs of heat illness;
f) Permitting modification of work schedules and reduced hours during high-heat days;
g) Providing education and training on heat illness prevention.

Employees should immediately report symptoms of heat-related illness, including excessive fatigue, weakness, nausea, dizziness, confusion, rapid or weak pulse, or red, hot, dry skin, to their supervisor or seek medical attention.

OSHA Compliance and Regulatory Standards. The Company maintains a comprehensive OSHA compliance program and complies with all applicable safety regulations. Employees are required to comply with all OSHA standards, Company safety policies, and the direction of supervisors regarding safe work practices. The Company provides training on workplace hazards, proper use of equipment, electrical safety, ladder safety, equipment operation, and other topics appropriate to employees' positions.

Injury Reporting. Any workplace injury or illness, regardless of severity or whether medical treatment is sought, must be reported immediately to your supervisor. Prompt reporting is critical for:

a) Ensuring that injured employees receive appropriate medical care;
b) Documenting injuries for worker's compensation claims;
c) Complying with OSHA recordkeeping requirements;
d) Investigating incidents and preventing future injuries;
e) Preserving evidence and witness statements.

Failure to report injuries or making a false injury report is grounds for disciplinary action.

Hazard Reporting. Employees who observe unsafe conditions, potential hazards, or violations of safety policies are required to report them immediately to their supervisor or safety personnel. Examples of reportable hazards include:

a) Damaged equipment or tools;
b) Slippery or obstructed walkways;
c) Unsafe storage of materials or chemicals;
d) Malfunctioning safety equipment;
e) Inadequate ventilation;
f) Temperature extremes;
g) Pest infestation;
h) Other unsafe conditions.

The Company will investigate all reported hazards and take corrective action. Employees are protected from retaliation for reporting hazards in good faith.

12. DRUG AND ALCOHOL TESTING

Purpose. The Company is committed to maintaining a safe, drug-free, and alcohol-free workplace. All employees are required to comply with the Company's drug and alcohol policy and testing procedures.

Policy. The use, possession, distribution, or sale of illegal drugs, controlled substances, or alcohol on Company premises, in Company vehicles, or during work hours is strictly prohibited. Employees who violate this policy are subject to immediate disciplinary action up to and including termination.

Pre-Employment Testing. All employment offers are contingent upon the applicant passing a drug screening test. Drug screening typically involves a five-panel urine test screening for the presence of marijuana, cocaine, amphetamines, opioids, and phencyclidine (PCP). Applicants who test positive will not be hired. Applicants who refuse to test will be disqualified from employment.

Department of Transportation (DOT) Compliance. Employees who operate commercial vehicles in the performance of their job duties are subject to Department of Transportation (DOT) federal regulations regarding drug and alcohol testing. These employees are required to:

a) Pass pre-employment drug and alcohol testing;
b) Submit to post-accident testing following reportable accidents;
c) Participate in random drug and alcohol testing;
d) Submit to reasonable suspicion testing;
e) Complete return-to-duty testing if they test positive;
f) Maintain a valid commercial driver's license (CDL);
g) Comply with all DOT safety regulations and Hours of Service requirements.

DOT-regulated employees are subject to random testing at least once per calendar year. The Company maintains a database of DOT-testing-required employees and conducts testing in accordance with DOT guidelines.

Reasonable Suspicion Testing. Any employee may be required to submit to drug and alcohol testing if the Company develops reasonable suspicion that the employee is under the influence of illegal drugs or alcohol while at work or before work. Reasonable suspicion may be based on:

a) Direct observation of physical symptoms consistent with drug or alcohol use;
b) Erratic or irrational behavior;
c) Violation of safety policies or procedures;
d) Admission by the employee;
e) Positive results from other testing;
f) Reliable information from witnesses.

Supervisors will not make reasonable suspicion determinations based solely on speculation or circumstantial evidence.

Random Testing. The Company maintains a random drug and alcohol testing program for designated positions and employee classifications. Employees selected for random testing will be notified and required to undergo testing within a specified timeframe. Random selection is conducted using a neutral, objective method.

Testing Procedures. All drug and alcohol testing is conducted in accordance with applicable DOT regulations and Company policy. Testing is performed by a certified laboratory that follows chain-of-custody procedures. Tests include:

a) Initial screening test;
b) Confirmation testing if the initial test is positive;
c) Medical review by a physician.

Employees have the right to request a confirmation test at their own expense if they believe a positive result is inaccurate. A split sample is retained for employee-initiated testing.

Positive Results and Consequences. Employees who test positive for illegal drugs or alcohol are subject to disciplinary action up to and including immediate termination of employment. Positive results are reported to:

a) The Company's medical review officer;
b) Human Resources and management;
c) Appropriate regulatory agencies (for DOT-regulated employees);
d) Law enforcement, if applicable.

Rehabilitation and Return-to-Work. Employees who test positive may be offered the opportunity to participate in an employee assistance program (EAP) or substance abuse treatment program. Participation is voluntary but may be a condition of continued employment. Employees who complete treatment and pass return-to-duty testing may be reinstated to employment at the Company's discretion. However, employees in safety-sensitive positions may not return to such positions without passing return-to-duty testing and a physical examination.

Confidentiality. Drug and alcohol test results are confidential and are maintained in secure personnel files separate from other employment records. Results are disclosed only to authorized personnel, including supervisors, Human Resources, and regulatory agencies as required by law.

Confidentiality Obligations. All employees are required to:

a) Keep confidential information strictly confidential at all times;
b) Not disclose confidential information to anyone outside the Company, including family members, friends, or competitors;
c) Not remove confidential information from Company premises without authorization;
d) Secure confidential information and prevent unauthorized access or disclosure;
e) Not use confidential information for personal benefit or for the benefit of any other person or entity;
f) Immediately report any unauthorized disclosure or security breach;
g) Return all confidential information upon separation of employment.

These confidentiality obligations continue indefinitely after employment ends.

Customer Information. Meridian has invested substantial time, money, and effort in acquiring and maintaining customer relationships. Customers are exclusive Company customers, and our customer lists are valuable, proprietary assets. Employees may not:

a) Solicit or accept business from Company customers for personal gain or benefit;
b) Encourage or persuade Company customers to use competitors' services;
c) Provide or furnish customer information, customer lists, or customer contact information to competitors or other unauthorized persons;
d) Conduct business transactions or service calls for Company customers without authorization;
e) Use our customer information to establish independent businesses or compete with the Company;
f) Take customer contact information when leaving employment.

Violation of customer confidentiality may result in criminal prosecution and substantial civil liability.

Protection of Confidential Information. Employees should:

a) Keep confidential documents locked in secure locations;
b) Not leave confidential information unattended or visible;
c) Destroy confidential documents appropriately (shredding, burning, etc.) when no longer needed;
d) Use secure passwords and do not share passwords;
e) Log off computers when not in use;
f) Not discuss confidential information in public or where others can overhear;
g) Not photograph or record confidential information;
h) Report suspected security breaches immediately.

15. TECHNOLOGY USE, COMPUTERS, AND DATA SECURITY

Company Systems and Equipment. Meridian provides computers, laptops, mobile devices, software, email accounts, internet access, and other technology to employees for business purposes. All Company technology and data are Company property. Employees should have no expectation of privacy when using Company technology or networks.

Authorized Use. Company technology may be used only for authorized business purposes and for occasional personal use that does not interfere with work responsibilities. Employees are prohibited from:

a) Using Company technology to access, create, or transmit obscene, offensive, or discriminatory materials;
b) Using Company email, internet, or systems for significant personal purposes unrelated to work;
c) Accessing unauthorized, adult, gambling, or illegal websites;
d) Downloading or installing unauthorized software or applications;
e) Circumventing security systems or attempting to gain unauthorized access;
f) Sending threatening, harassing, or abusive messages;
g) Visiting social media sites for non-work purposes;
h) Recording or photographing without authorization;
i) Creating, viewing, or distributing child exploitation materials or images, which will be immediately reported to law enforcement;
j) Sharing passwords or allowing others to use accounts;
k) Removing or borrowing Company technology without authorization.

Monitoring and Surveillance. The Company may monitor, access, and review all electronic communications and data on Company systems, including email, internet usage, text messages, chat messages, browsing history, and stored documents, without notice or consent. Monitoring is conducted to:

a) Ensure compliance with Company policies;
b) Protect confidential and proprietary information;
c) Investigate misconduct or rule violations;
d) Ensure productivity;
e) Prevent security threats;
f) Comply with legal and regulatory requirements.

Employees have no reasonable expectation of privacy when using Company technology.

Email and Communications. Email is business correspondence and is monitored by the Company. Employees should not send personal emails of a confidential nature using Company email. Email messages may be recovered and reviewed by the Company at any time. Inappropriate email content, including sexual material, discriminatory content, or threats, may result in disciplinary action.

Security and Data Protection. Employees are responsible for maintaining the security of Company data and preventing unauthorized access. Employees must:

a) Create strong passwords and change passwords regularly;
b) Not write down passwords or share them with others;
c) Log off computers when leaving the workstation;
d) Use screen privacy filters when working in public areas;
e) Not leave documents or devices unattended;
f) Report suspicious emails or phishing attempts;
g) Report lost or stolen devices immediately;
h) Comply with all data security protocols;
i) Not remove Company data from Company premises without authorization.

16. SOCIAL MEDIA

Personal Social Media Accounts. Employees' personal social media accounts and public statements, even when made outside of work hours, may reflect on Meridian and can impact the Company's reputation and customer relationships. While the Company respects employees' personal social media use, employees are required to:

a) Not disclose confidential Company information, customer data, or trade secrets on social media;
b) Not make disparaging, derogatory, or negative comments about the Company, Company leadership, coworkers, or customers on social media;
c) Clarify that personal opinions and statements on social media are personal opinions and do not represent the views or policies of the Company;
d) Not conduct Company business or accept customer service requests through personal social media accounts without authorization;
e) Maintain professionalism and decorum in all public communications;
f) Immediately notify the Company of any posts or comments that could harm the Company's reputation;
g) Cooperate with the Company in removing inappropriate posts or clarifying misunderstandings.

Company Social Media. Employees designated to manage Company social media accounts must:

a) Follow all Company policies and brand guidelines;
b) Maintain a professional tone;
c) Not make unauthorized statements on behalf of the Company;
d) Protect login credentials;
e) Report to supervisors regarding social media activity;
f) Not disclose confidential information through social media.

Social Media Harassment. Social media harassment of coworkers, customers, or others is prohibited and may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination.

17. CUSTOMER-HOME CONDUCT

Field Technician Responsibilities. Field technicians regularly work in customers' homes and businesses. Employees must conduct themselves professionally, ethically, and respectfully at all times while at customer locations. Customer satisfaction and trust are essential to our business success.

Professional Appearance and Conduct. Employees must:

a) Wear clean, professional uniforms and appropriate footwear;
b) Maintain good personal hygiene;
c) Be courteous, respectful, and professional with all customers;
d) Listen carefully to customer concerns and questions;
e) Explain work clearly in understandable language;
f) Provide accurate estimates and pricing information;
g) Minimize disruption to the customer's home or business;
h) Protect customer property and belongings;
i) Clean up work areas and remove debris;
j) Respect customer privacy and personal space;
k) Follow all customer directives regarding entry to home, work areas, and restricted areas;
l) Complete work in a timely manner;
m) Provide written documentation of work completed.

Prohibited Conduct. The following conduct is strictly prohibited:

a) Entering areas of the customer's home or business without permission, including bedrooms, bathrooms, private offices, or storage areas;
b) Moving, using, or touching customer property without authorization;
c) Photographing or recording customers or the customer's home without permission;
d) Discussing customer information, pricing, service history, or other customer data with anyone;
e) Engaging in conversation of a sexual, harassing, or discriminatory nature;
f) Making inappropriate comments about the customer's appearance, home, family, or possessions;
g) Soliciting customers for personal services, jobs, or business unrelated to Meridian;
h) Discussing Company business, other customers, or employees with the customer;
i) Using profanity, inappropriate language, or raising voice;
j) Consuming alcohol or appearing under the influence of alcohol or drugs;
k) Smoking, vaping, or using tobacco products on customer premises;
l) Leaving work sites in disrepair, creating hazards, or leaving tools or materials;
m) Taking photographs, documents, or items from the customer's location;
n) Engaging in behavior that makes customers feel uncomfortable or unsafe;
o) Failing to wear uniforms or safety equipment.

Violation of these customer conduct standards may result in immediate termination.

Dating and Personal Relationships. Employees are not permitted to initiate or pursue personal or romantic relationships with customers. Such relationships create conflicts of interest and may be perceived as inappropriate. Employees who develop personal relationships with customers must immediately disclose this to their supervisor so that service assignments can be adjusted.

19. SEPARATION OF EMPLOYMENT

Notice and Resignation. Employees who wish to resign from employment are requested to provide written notice to their supervisor and Human Resources Department at least two weeks in advance. This notice period allows the Company to arrange for a replacement and ensure proper transition of work. While the Company requests two weeks' notice, employees are not required to provide notice and may resign effective immediately.

Exit Interview. Upon separation of employment, Human Resources will conduct an exit interview with the departing employee. The exit interview provides an opportunity to discuss the employee's experience with the Company and gather feedback regarding policies, management, and working conditions.

Return of Company Property. Upon separation, employees must immediately return all Company property, including:

a) Company vehicles and keys;
b) Tools and equipment;
c) Uniforms and safety gear;
d) Computers, laptops, mobile devices, and peripherals;
e) Company credit cards;
f) Access badges and security cards;
g) Company documents and files;
h) Any other Company property.

Employees are responsible for any lost or damaged Company property.

Confidentiality Obligations. Upon separation, employees remain bound by all confidentiality and trade secret obligations. These obligations continue indefinitely for trade secrets that qualify for legal protection and for such periods as specified in restrictive covenant agreements and applicable law.

Final Paycheck. The Company will process a final paycheck within the timeframe required by applicable law. The final paycheck will include:

a) All earned but unpaid wages;
b) Accrued and unused paid time off (where required by law);
c) Prorated bonuses or commissions (if applicable and earned);
d) Reimbursement for approved business expenses.

The Company may deduct from the final paycheck any amounts owed to the Company for equipment, tools, uniforms, or other property that has not been returned, subject to applicable state law.

Benefits Continuation. Employees separated from employment may be eligible for continuation of health insurance coverage under the Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act (COBRA). Information regarding COBRA continuation coverage, eligibility, and how to elect continuation will be provided in a separate notice.

References. Employment references are provided only through the Company's Human Resources Department. Supervisors and coworkers should not provide individual references; all reference requests should be directed to HR. The Company provides only minimal job-related information, including dates of employment, job title, and eligibility for rehire.

Retaliation Prohibition. The Company does not retaliate against employees for resigning employment or for any lawful action taken by the employee.
