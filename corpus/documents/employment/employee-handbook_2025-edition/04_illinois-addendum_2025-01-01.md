ADDENDUM TO EMPLOYEE HANDBOOK
MERIDIAN MECHANICAL OF ILLINOIS, LLC
ILLINOIS EMPLOYMENT LAWS COMPLIANCE

EFFECTIVE DATE: JANUARY 1, 2025

This addendum to the Employee Handbook of Meridian Mechanical of Illinois, LLC ("Company"), with principal offices located at 1847 North Clark Street, Chicago, Illinois 60614, is adopted and incorporated into the Employee Handbook, effective January 1, 2025. This addendum addresses compliance with several Illinois state employment laws that apply to all employees of the Company.

1. PAID LEAVE FOR ALL WORKERS

In compliance with the Illinois Paid Leave for All Workers Act, 820 ILCS 195/1 et seq., the Company provides paid leave to all employees employed for at least ninety (90) days.

1.1 Accrual and Rate

Employees accrue paid leave at a rate of one (1) hour per thirty (30) hours worked. For purposes of accrual calculation, "hours worked" includes all compensated time, including regular hours, overtime, and paid time off. Alternatively, the Company may provide a minimum of two (2) weeks of paid leave per twelve (12) month period to all employees who work a standard full-time schedule of forty (40) hours per week or more.

1.2 Use of Paid Leave

Paid leave may be used for any reason, including but not limited to personal illness, illness of a family member requiring care, medical or dental treatment, preventive care, school or childcare facility closures, public health emergencies, domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, or any other reason employees deem appropriate. Employees are not required to disclose the specific reason for which they are using paid leave, except as otherwise required by law or court order. The Company respects employee privacy and will maintain the confidentiality of information provided regarding use of paid leave.

1.3 Scheduling and Approval

Employees must request paid leave in accordance with the Company's normal call-in procedures and as far in advance as practicable. In the case of unscheduled or emergency absences, employees should notify their supervisor or the main office at (312) 555-0123 as soon as possible, but in no case later than two (2) hours before the start of the scheduled shift. The Company will make reasonable efforts to accommodate employee requests for planned use of paid leave. Unscheduled absences due to illness or emergency will be charged against accrued paid leave unless otherwise agreed by the Company.

1.4 Carryover and Accrual Limits

During each twelve (12) month period beginning January 1, employees may carry over up to forty (40) hours of unused paid leave to the next twelve (12) month period. Paid leave balances in excess of forty (40) hours at the end of a twelve (12) month period shall be paid out at the employee's regular rate of pay, or at the Company's discretion, the employee's accrual rate may be reset to zero for the subsequent period, provided that the employee retains a minimum carryover of forty (40) hours. In no case shall the accrual of paid leave be capped or limited in a manner that prevents employees from accruing leave in accordance with this policy.

1.5 Compensation Upon Separation of Employment

Upon termination of employment for any reason, including voluntary resignation, discharge, layoff, retirement, or death, the Company shall compensate employees for all accrued and unused paid leave at the employee's regular rate of pay in effect at the time of separation. Such compensation shall be paid within all wages due at the time of the employee's final paycheck, or as otherwise required by law. Final paychecks will be issued in accordance with the Illinois Wage Payment Act.

1.6 Victims' Economic Security and Safety Act

In compliance with the Victims' Economic Security and Safety Act, 820 ILCS 180/1 et seq., employees who are victims of domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking may use accrued paid leave to address matters arising from such circumstances. Such purposes include, but are not limited to, seeking legal assistance or remedies; obtaining medical care or mental health counseling; securing safe housing or emergency relocation; acquiring safety equipment or professional services; and attending court proceedings or meetings with law enforcement, legal counsel, social services, or healthcare providers.

Employees may use paid leave for purposes under this Act without disclosing the specific nature, details, or circumstances of the underlying offense, threat, or fear, except as required by law, court order, or when disclosure is necessary to comply with workplace safety obligations. The Company will not retaliate, threaten, intimidate, or take any adverse action against any employee for using paid leave in accordance with this provision or for disclosing information concerning domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking only to the extent necessary to receive workplace protections.

2. ONE DAY REST IN SEVEN

In compliance with the One Day Rest in Seven Act, 820 ILCS 140, the Company ensures that each employee receives at least one (1) day of rest in every seven (7) consecutive days.

2.1 Rest Days

Employees are entitled to at least one (1) complete day of rest, consisting of twenty-four (24) consecutive hours, in every seven (7) consecutive calendar days. The Company will make reasonable efforts to provide rest days on the same day of the week whenever practicable, consistent with business operations and customer service requirements. Scheduling decisions will take into account employee requests and established work schedules.

2.2 Compensation for Work on Rest Days

Employees who are required or scheduled to work on their designated rest day shall receive compensation for such work in accordance with applicable wage and hour laws. Employees who voluntarily work on a designated rest day shall receive their regular rate of pay for all hours worked. Time and one-half compensation for work on rest days shall be provided only if such work also results in overtime compensation under the Fair Labor Standards Act or Illinois wage and hour laws.

2.3 Emergencies and Exceptions

The requirements of this provision may be suspended only in the event of genuine emergencies that could not reasonably have been anticipated or prevented, including but not limited to fire, flood, major equipment malfunction, utility outages, acts of God, or other extraordinary circumstances where cessation of business operations would result in substantial physical damage or injury to persons or property. Any such emergency suspension must be documented and reported to the employee in writing within one (1) business day and to the Department of Labor within forty-eight (48) hours. The Company will provide the requisite rest period at the earliest practicable time following resolution of the emergency.

3. HARASSMENT PREVENTION TRAINING

In compliance with the Illinois Human Rights Act, 775 ILCS 5/1 et seq., and to maintain a respectful workplace free from harassment and discrimination, the Company requires annual harassment prevention training for all employees.

3.1 Training Requirements

All employees of Meridian Mechanical of Illinois, LLC must complete harassment prevention training during each calendar year of employment. New employees hired after January 1 in any given year must complete such training within thirty (30) days of hire, or by December 31 of the same calendar year, whichever occurs first. Employees on leave or temporary separation will be required to complete training upon their return to active employment.

3.2 Training Content

The Company-provided training program covers the following topics:

(a) Recognition of and definition of harassment based on protected characteristics under the Illinois Human Rights Act, including race, color, religion, sex (including pregnancy, sexual orientation, and gender identity), national origin, ancestry, age, disability, marital status, military status, order of protection status, genetic information, and unfavorable discharge from military service;

(b) Prevention of harassment in the workplace, including in-person, electronic, and social media contexts;

(c) The Company's zero-tolerance policy toward harassment, discrimination, and related retaliation;

(d) Procedures for reporting harassment, discrimination, and retaliation, including the specific individuals or departments designated to receive complaints and alternative reporting channels;

(e) The legal protections afforded to employees who report harassment, discrimination, or participate in investigations, including protections against retaliation;

(f) Bystander intervention and safe, supportive responses to witnessed harassment or discrimination;

(g) Resources available to employees who experience harassment or discrimination, including internal and external support services; and

(h) The investigation and resolution process for complaints of harassment or discrimination.

3.3 Training Delivery and Documentation

Training will be delivered through online interactive modules, in-person sessions conducted by qualified trainers, or other methods approved by the Company that ensure interactive participation and comprehension. Each employee must acknowledge receipt and completion of the training by signing a training roster, submitting electronic confirmation, or other documented form of acknowledgment. The Company maintains records of all training completions and acknowledgments in the employee's personnel file and retains such records for a minimum of three (3) years.

3.4 Reporting Harassment and Discrimination

Employees who experience or witness harassment, discrimination, retaliation, or any conduct that violates the Illinois Human Rights Act should report it immediately to their supervisor, the Human Resources Manager, Maria Gonzalez, at (312) 555-0147 or hr@meridianmechanical.com, or through an alternative reporting channel if reporting to the supervisor creates a conflict. The Company will investigate all reports promptly, thoroughly, and as confidentially as possible given the need to investigate. The Company will not retaliate against any employee for making a good-faith report of harassment or discrimination or for participating in an investigation.

4. RESTRICTIVE COVENANTS

The Company is committed to compliance with the Illinois Freedom to Work Act, 820 ILCS 90/1 et seq., concerning restrictive covenants and non-compete agreements.

4.1 Applicability of Restrictive Covenants

Any restrictive covenant or similar provision included in an employment agreement, non-compete agreement, non-solicitation agreement, confidentiality agreement, or other restrictive agreement entered into with an employee of Meridian Mechanical of Illinois, LLC is subject to and governed by the Illinois Freedom to Work Act. Such provisions shall be enforceable only to the extent they comply with that Act.

4.2 Earnings Threshold Requirements

Restrictive covenants, including but not limited to covenants not to compete and covenants not to solicit customers or employees, shall be enforceable against an employee only if that employee's monthly rate of compensation, exclusive of incentive-based payments, bonuses, and benefits, was at least:

(a) Seventy-five thousand dollars ($75,000) during the most recent twelve (12) months of employment, if the employee's principal place of employment is in Cook County;

(b) Sixty thousand dollars ($60,000) during the most recent twelve (12) months of employment, if the employee's principal place of employment is in a county other than Cook County with a population exceeding 100,000 according to the most recent federal census; or

(c) Forty thousand dollars ($40,000) during the most recent twelve (12) months of employment, if the employee's principal place of employment is in any other location in Illinois.

These threshold amounts are adjusted annually by the Illinois Secretary of State. The currently applicable threshold amounts are available from the Human Resources Department.

4.3 Reasonableness Standards

Any restrictive covenant the Company seeks to enforce must satisfy the following reasonableness standards:

(a) Time Period. The duration of the restriction must be reasonable in light of the legitimate business interests to be protected. A covenant not to compete that restricts an employee for a period not exceeding two (2) years from the date of separation of employment is presumed to be reasonable as to duration.

(b) Geographic Area. The geographic scope of the restriction must be limited to the relevant market area in which the Company conducted business or had established legitimate business relationships during the employee's employment. Overly broad geographic restrictions shall not be enforceable.

(c) Line of Business. The restriction must be limited to the specific type or line of business engaged in by the Company at the time the restrictive covenant was entered into. Restrictions covering broader categories of business than the Company actually conducted shall not be enforceable.

4.4 Required Notice Regarding Restrictive Covenants

Any employee who is required to sign a restrictive covenant, or who is presented with such an agreement during employment, must be provided with written notice that:

(a) Any restrictive covenant is void and unenforceable unless the employee earned at least the applicable monthly compensation threshold set forth in Section 4.2 above during the most recent twelve (12) months of employment;

(b) If an employee does not meet or exceed the applicable earnings threshold, the restrictive covenant cannot be enforced by the Company and is not binding on the employee;

(c) Employees are entitled to consult with an attorney or other legal advisor regarding the enforceability of any restrictive covenant;

(d) The Company will not threaten an employee with enforcement of a restrictive covenant that the Company knows or should know is unenforceable under the Illinois Freedom to Work Act; and

(e) Employees will not be subject to retaliation or adverse employment action for refusing to sign a restrictive covenant that does not comply with the requirements of the Illinois Freedom to Work Act.

4.5 Reformation

To the extent any restrictive covenant is found to be overbroad or unreasonable in time, area, or scope, the parties consent that such provision may be reformed by a court of competent jurisdiction to make it reasonable and enforceable, provided that such reformation may only occur if the employee earned at least the applicable threshold compensation amount set forth in Section 4.2 above during the relevant time period.

5. EMPLOYEE RIGHTS AND LEGAL PROTECTIONS

The Company is committed to maintaining a workplace free from retaliation and to protecting all employee rights under applicable federal and state law. Nothing in this handbook, including this addendum, is intended to limit, restrict, or interfere with any employee's rights, including but not limited to the right to discuss wages, hours, and other terms and conditions of employment. In accordance with the National Labor Relations Act and other applicable law, employees are protected in their right to engage in concerted activity for mutual aid and protection.
